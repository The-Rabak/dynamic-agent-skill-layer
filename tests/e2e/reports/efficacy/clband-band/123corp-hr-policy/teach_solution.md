The HR policy document doesn't state the pay frequency in plain language, but we can work it out precisely from the vacation accrual rates in Section 7.01.

**The derivation:**

Each tier of the vacation table gives both an annual amount and a per-pay-period accrual rate. Dividing the annual hours by the per-pay-period rate tells us how many pay periods there are in a year:

| Tenure | Annual vacation | Per-pay-period rate | Pay periods/year |
|--------|----------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| 0–1 year | 5 days = 40 hrs | 1.54 hrs | 40 ÷ 1.54 ≈ **26** |
| 2–4 years | 10 days = 80 hrs | 3.08 hrs | 80 ÷ 3.08 ≈ **26** |
| 5–9 years | 15 days = 120 hrs | 4.62 hrs | 120 ÷ 4.62 ≈ **26** |
| 10+ years | 20 days = 160 hrs | 6.15 hrs | 160 ÷ 6.15 ≈ **26** |

All four tiers converge on **26 pay periods per year**, which corresponds to a **biweekly** (every two weeks) pay schedule.

**Answer: You are paid every two weeks (biweekly).**

Section 7.02 (Sick Leave) independently confirms this structure — sick leave also accrues at "2 hours each pay period," which over 26 pay periods yields 52 hours per year, a reasonable annual sick leave bank.

One practical note from Section 7.01: vacation hours are *credited at the completion of each pay period* and you become *eligible to use earned vacation after three months of employment* — so your first few paychecks will build up a vacation balance you can start drawing on around month four.

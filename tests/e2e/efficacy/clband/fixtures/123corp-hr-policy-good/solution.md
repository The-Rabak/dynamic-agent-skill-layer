Starting a new job is a lot to take on all at once, so it's totally understandable to feel a bit overwhelmed. Let me tackle your questions one at a time.

**Do you need a doctor's note if you call in sick?**

It depends on the situation. According to section 7.02 of the company policy, when you return from sick leave, your manager *may* require you to submit a written statement of medical certification explaining the nature of your illness or disability. So it's not always mandatory, but your manager has the right to ask for it.

For anything that required actual medical attention — a doctor's visit, urgent care, that sort of thing — you'll need to provide a letter from your health care provider that includes the **diagnosis**, the **prognosis**, and any work limitations you'll need accommodated when you come back. So for a serious situation, yes, you'll definitely need that documentation.

**How long until you have a week of sick pay?**

Here's the math: full-time employees accrue **2 hours of sick leave per pay period** starting from your hire date, per section 7.02. A full work week is 40 hours, so you'll need to accumulate 40 hours of sick leave. At 2 hours per pay period, that takes 20 pay periods — which works out to roughly **10 months** if you're on a bi-weekly pay schedule.

So plan on having a full week of sick pay available after about 10 months on the job.
